ChatGPT can help turn notes about a photograph into a caption, product description, or gallery introduction. But a draft still needs attention before it belongs beside an image on your website. It may describe something the picture does not show, introduce an unsupported detail, or carry awkward spacing when you copy it into an editor.

A useful publishing workflow handles meaning and formatting separately. First, make the words accurate. Then clean the text and check the result in its final layout.

## Start with the image and its source

Before editing a caption, establish what you know about the image. Record the creator, source page, and any attribution requirements that apply to your intended use. If you only have a downloaded copy, our [guide to finding image origins](/blog/find-image-origin-guide) can help you investigate where it appeared.

A matching search result is a lead to inspect, rather than proof of authorship or permission. Keep a record of the source and any permission you obtain. That gives you something dependable to refer to when reviewing AI-assisted copy.

Next, separate visible facts from background information. A picture might show a market stall with baskets of tomatoes. It does not necessarily establish the city, date, seller's name, or whether the produce is organic. Supply confirmed details yourself and remove guesses from the draft.

## Give ChatGPT a narrow caption task

A specific request makes a draft easier to review. Include the intended audience, the image's role, a length limit, and the facts the caption may use.

For example, you could ask: “Write a caption of no more than 25 words for a photograph showing red tomatoes in woven baskets at an outdoor stall. Do not invent a location, date, or person's name.”

Compare the response with the photograph. Remove unnecessary claims, repeated adjectives, and generic introductions. If the caption introduces information that came from a source rather than the image, check that source before keeping it.

## Clean copied text before pasting it into your CMS

Once the wording is correct, look at the text itself. Copying between chat interfaces, documents, and website editors can leave extra blank lines, unusual spaces, or characters that are difficult to spot. These can make a short caption wrap strangely or behave differently from surrounding copy.

You can use [gpt clean up](https://gptcleanuptools.com) to tidy hidden characters and spacing in copied AI text before moving it into your publishing editor. Keep the original draft available, review the cleaned output, and confirm that paragraph breaks and punctuation still suit your caption.

For a short caption, pasting as plain text may also be sufficient. The important step is checking the result, whichever method you use. Formatting cleanup does not verify a location, correct an invented product feature, or establish permission to reuse an image.

## Keep captions, alt text, and credits distinct

These three pieces of text serve different purposes. A caption gives readers useful context beside an image. Alt text provides a text alternative based on the image's purpose on the page. A credit identifies the creator or source where appropriate or required.

For a product page, a caption could explain that a pictured bag includes a detachable shoulder strap. Alt text might describe the bag's colour and the angle shown. A credit belongs in a suitable attribution location if the photograph's terms require one.

Do not paste the same paragraph into every field automatically. Review each field in context, and avoid filling alt text with keywords that do not help explain the image. Our [image optimization guide](/blog/how-to-optimize-images-for-search) offers further context for preparing images and their accompanying text.

## Review a concrete before-and-after example

Suppose an AI draft reads: “Discover this stunning, award-winning handmade leather bag, crafted in Lagos for every adventure.” Your notes confirm only that the image shows a brown bag with a shoulder strap. The award, material, manufacturing method, and location all need evidence before publication.

A more careful caption would be: “Brown bag shown with its shoulder strap attached.” If the product specification confirms that the strap is detachable, you can add that detail. The useful improvement comes from checking the facts and choosing precise words.

After that edit, remove any unwanted blank lines or spacing and paste the caption into its field. Compare the saved version with your reviewed draft. This keeps a formatting operation from becoming a substitute for editorial judgment.

## Preview the image and caption together

Open the finished page on a narrow screen as well as a desktop layout. Check whether the caption sits beneath the correct image, whether credits remain readable, and whether long text crowds the gallery controls.

If the caption includes a source link, click it and confirm it opens the intended page. Check special characters, names, and product measurements after saving. Editors sometimes transform pasted text, so the final preview matters more than how the text looked before you pasted it.

For a batch of product images, publish a small sample first. Review several types of captions before applying the workflow to the entire catalogue. Keep original drafts and source notes so that corrections are straightforward later.

## A repeatable publishing routine

For each image, confirm the source, list the facts you can support, and review the draft against those facts. Clean the copied text, place captions and credits in their appropriate fields, and preview the saved page.

This routine gives AI-assisted captions a clear path from draft to publication. Your source checks establish accuracy; text cleanup makes the reviewed copy easier to move between tools; the final preview confirms that readers see the intended result.
